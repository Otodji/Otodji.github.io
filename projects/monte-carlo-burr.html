Beyond a line-by-line translation

🎯

Closed-form quantile

I derived the exact inverse of \(F\), replacing a bisection search: instant, exact, and used to validate the original method.

🧮

Stable log-likelihood

The log-density is computed term by term with log1p / expm1, so the likelihood stays finite in the tails.

📏

Standard errors

Uncertainty from the inverse Hessian of the negative log-likelihood, the Python equivalent of R's vcov().

🧪

Three GoF tests

Kolmogorov-Smirnov, Cramér-von Mises and Anderson-Darling, each sensitive to a different kind of misfit.

🔁

Parametric bootstrap

Corrected p-values: simulate from the fitted law, re-estimate, recompute the statistics.

✅

Built-in checks

The density integrates to 1 and matches the derivative of \(F\); simulated histograms match the theory.

Key derivation

Inverting the distribution exactly

Writing \(v = e^{u} \in (0, 1]\), the distribution function becomes \(F = \big(v(2 - v)\big)^m\), which inverts in closed form:

$$v = 1 - \sqrt{1 - U^{1/m}}, \quad K = \Big(1 - \frac{\ln v}{n}\Big)^{-1/p}, \quad G = \Big(\frac{-\ln(1 - K)}{\lambda}\Big)^{1/\beta}, \quad x = \Big((1 + G)^{1/k} - 1\Big)^{1/c}$$

If \(U \sim \mathcal{U}(0,1)\), then \(x = F^{-1}(U)\) follows the Burr-PTLEN distribution. This is the formula that powers the simulation below.

Lessons from the study

ValidatedSimulated histograms match the theoretical density, and bisection agrees with the exact quantile.
IdentifiabilityWith 7 parameters and 250 points, different parameter sets give nearly identical curves: estimates can drift while the fit stays excellent.
Honest p-valuesTesting on the data used for fitting makes classical p-values too large. The parametric bootstrap gives the p-values to trust.
